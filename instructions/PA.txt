## READ FIRST — resumability

Sessions here end abruptly when usage runs out, with no warning and no chance to tidy up. Assume
this session will be cut off mid-step. Your job is to make that survivable.

**`instructions/RESUME.md` is the operational cursor.** Read it before anything else — it records
which steps below are `done`, `in-progress` or `not-started`, the last green commit, and facts
already observed. Follow its cold-start protocol. Do not re-derive anything it marks `done`; do not
trust anything it marks `in-progress` without re-verifying that step's outputs.

While working:

* **Commit at every checkpoint marked `>>> CHECKPOINT` below.** Never let more than one
  checkpoint's work sit uncommitted.
* Use `wip(handoff): <what landed>` for intermediate commits. They are expected to be non-green —
  that is fine and expected. Push every one; an unpushed commit is not durable.
* Update `RESUME.md` in the *same* commit as the work: flip the step to `done`, record any observed
  counts or SHAs in the facts table, and rewrite the **Next action** line so a cold start knows
  exactly where to pick up.
* Before starting A3 or A5 (the two long steps), mark them `in-progress` in `RESUME.md` and commit
  that first.
* At the end, A6 replaces the accumulated `wip` commits with one final green commit on top. Do not
  rewrite pushed history to squash them; just land the green commit and note in `RESUME.md` which
  SHA is the green one.
* Do not run the full A3 preflight at every checkpoint — it is expensive. Run the cheap subset
  (`check_doc_consistency.py`, `check_literals.py`, targeted `pytest -k`) mid-flight and the full
  block once at A6.

If you cold-start into this file with `RESUME.md` still saying "PA — not started" but `git log`
showing `wip(handoff)` commits, trust `git log`: reconstruct the true state from the diffs, fix
`RESUME.md` first, commit that, then continue.
